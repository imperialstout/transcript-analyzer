# Daily, Weekly, and Career Synthesis Prompts

This file lives in your `Workcall/` folder in Google Drive, alongside `PromptLibrary.md`.
The analyzer reads it at runtime — editing it here takes effect on the next synthesis run.

Each prompt uses the same `### KEY.` + fenced block convention as `PromptLibrary.md`.
Keys are D1 (daily), D2 (weekly), D3 (career). All three are required if you run the
corresponding mode; the run fails with a clear error if a key is missing.

---

### D1.

```
You are producing a Daily Pulse — a fast end-of-day brief the reader writes for
themselves. The audience is the reader alone. Be specific, attributed, and candid.
Omit sections that have nothing to report.

## TL;DR
3–5 bullets: what actually moved today, what arrived on the radar, what needs the
reader tomorrow. No filler — if it's not actionable or signal-bearing, cut it.

## By workstream
For each workstream with activity today: one tight paragraph. Progress made,
commitments given or received, blockers that appeared or moved. Name who did what.

## Decisions and commitments made today
Bullet list: decision or commitment — owner — deadline or "unspecified".
Flag anything provisional, softened, or walked back.

## Things that need the reader tomorrow
Explicit asks: who is waiting on the reader, for what, by when. Don't bury these.

## Watch list
Soft signals, early-stage risks, and things that didn't resolve. Keep it short —
three items maximum. If nothing belongs here, omit the section.

## Private read — internal only
The political and career layer for today. What was read between the lines, who
shifted stance, what the reader's own positioning looks like right now. Be direct —
this is the internal intelligence layer and no one else will read it.
```

### D2.

```
You are producing a Weekly Slack Delta — a tight, paste-ready update the reader can
send to their team or leadership at the end of the week. Tone: confident, factual,
forward-looking. No hedging. No internal politics.

Keep the whole thing under 400 words. It should be readable in 90 seconds.

## Week of [dates]
One sentence framing the week's headline.

## Progress
3–5 bullets. What shipped, what moved, what closed. Concrete and attributed.

## In flight
2–4 bullets. Key workstreams still running, with current status and next milestone.

## Risks and blockers
2–3 bullets. Only the ones leadership needs to know about. If something is fully
mitigated, drop it.

## Coming up
2–3 bullets. What the reader is driving next week: decisions due, milestones, meetings
that matter.
```

### D3.

```
You are producing a Career and Position Trajectory review. The reader is a senior
delivery professional reflecting on where they stand — not just on the program, but
in the organization, in their career, and in the political landscape around them.

This is not a project status report. It is an honest, private strategic read.
Be direct. Be specific. Don't soften findings to protect the reader's ego.

## Executive position
How does the reader appear to senior stakeholders right now? Where is their credibility
strong? Where is it at risk or untested? What signals from the last period are most
relevant to their standing?

## Program narrative
How is the program being told upward — and is the reader controlling that narrative
or reacting to it? Where is the story strong? Where is it fragile or dependent on
things outside the reader's control?

## Political landscape
Who are the reader's allies, and are those relationships being maintained? Who is a
friction point, and is it being managed or ignored? Any new dynamics that changed the
map this period?

## Workload and sustainability
Is the reader operating at a sustainable level? What is the evidence — from the
transcripts — that the load is or isn't working? What is being dropped or deferred
that will matter later?

## Career signals
What opportunities, risks, or positioning moves are visible in this period's material?
What should the reader be doing differently to protect or advance their position?

## Recommended moves
3–5 concrete, specific actions the reader should take in the next two weeks based on
everything above. Not general advice — specific to this person's situation.
```
